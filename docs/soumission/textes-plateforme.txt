BIO / ONE-LINER

Sybil-resistant ERC-8004 reputation a contract can check before paying.


DESCRIPTION

ANCRE is a reputation layer for AI agents that a smart contract can read before it pays. It is built on ERC-8004, the standard that gives agents an on-chain identity and lets anyone leave feedback about them. ERC-8004 deliberately leaves one problem open. Anyone can rate any agent, for free, and the standard says in its own text that Sybil attacks are possible. Filtering is left to the reader: calling getSummary without a list of trusted raters simply reverts. The reader is expected to know whom to trust, but is given no tool to work it out. ANCRE is that tool. We do not build another registry or another agent passport. We build the layer the standard hands to the reader.

The problem is not theoretical. We re-read the canonical ERC-8004 registries on Monad mainnet directly on-chain. On 28 September 2026 they held 10,262 agents and 9,283 feedback entries. 83% of all that feedback points to a single agent, Eve-8004, and comes from 7,665 agents created in about 48 hours. Because 117 of those ratings are set to 1e38, Eve's naive average is 1.53 × 10^36. Any application that ranks agents by their average rating puts that agent first. Requiring proof of payment does not help: the farm paid, and the raters were funded by the same owner. A receipt can be true and still come from a farm. We ran the same analysis with Envio HyperSync on seven chains, and the pattern repeats everywhere. Less than 5% of ERC-8004 feedback survives as a usable trust signal, and on Monad only 0.12% does.

ANCRE starts from one idea: trust does not exist in the absolute, it exists from someone's point of view. There is no global score. The ERC-8004 authors themselves consider a single global score dangerous, and a post in the standard's discussion thread (ethereum-magicians #309) asked for exactly this approach, without an answer so far. Each reader, whether a company, a DAO or a buyer agent, chooses a small set of anchors it already trusts. ANCRE then computes Personalized PageRank, from the EigenTrust family, starting from those anchors and following the graph of ratings between agents. Trust only flows along ratings given by agents the anchors can reach. A farm of thousands of accounts rating each other therefore receives nothing, unless one of the reader's anchors vouches for it. Two readers can look at the same registry and see two different rankings, and that is intended.

The computation happens off-chain, but the result is certified on-chain. A solver submits a certificate containing the graph in calldata, the trust vector and an error bound. The AnchoredReputation contract recomputes one PageRank step and checks the residual. Since the PageRank map is a contraction, a small residual guarantees a lower bound on every agent's trust. That bound is classical; what is new is enforcing it inside a contract. At the strongest level, which we call Registry-verified, the contract also re-reads every edge from the live ERC-8004 registry, so a fabricated or inflated rating is rejected at submission. Omitting or diluting edges is still possible, and anyone can prove it on-chain afterwards. We present the guarantee with its level and its limits.

The certified score is then used where it matters: at payment time. TrustGatedPayer is a smart account (ERC-1271) that holds the USDC used to pay other agents over x402. Its signature check calls isTrusted on the ANCRE contract. If the provider is below the reader's threshold, or if one of the anchors has vetoed it, the payment cannot settle. The refusal comes from the contract, not from an interface that could be bypassed. When an anchor flags a provider that went bad, every payer relying on those anchors stops paying it from the next certificate onward. We also wrote a plugin for the MetaMask Agent Wallet, mm ancre, which checks trust before the agent signs anything. If ANCRE refuses, only a human can force the payment, from their own wallet, and the agent has no path to do it alone.

On the real Monad registry, with two anchors, Eve-8004 ends up with a trust mass of exactly 0, compared with its naive average of 1.53e36. The few honest ratings that remain form a short chain from the anchors to agent #18 and then to agents #2 and #3, which receive a positive, certified score. That honest graph is very small today, so ANCRE answers "unknown" for most agents. This is the correct answer, and the positive side of our demo relies on our own agent traffic.

Monad is what makes this practical. Blocks come every 0.3 seconds (we measured 0.302 s over 1,000 blocks), so once a new certificate lands, a veto stops payments from the very next block. We recertify on every change of the graph rather than on every block. In our fork tests, it took 390 ms from a graph change to the on-chain receipt. The Registry-verified certificate for the demo graph costs 14.97 million gas and fits in a single transaction. The trust check adds about 183,000 gas per payment. The canonical ERC-8004 registries are deployed on both Monad mainnet and testnet, so we work against the real ones. The standard we propose stays chain-agnostic, since ERC-8004 runs on more than 40 chains, but Monad is where its strongest verification level becomes routine.

The project includes the verifier and payer contracts in Solidity, tested with Foundry against forks of Monad mainnet and testnet. It also includes the PageRank solver and certification service in Python, an Envio HyperIndex indexer running on Envio Cloud for Monad mainnet, Monad testnet and Optimism, and the MetaMask Agent Wallet plugin. Alongside the code, we wrote a draft ERC, "Anchored Reputation for Trustless Agents", with test vectors and an independent verifier. ANCRE is meant to be its reference implementation.

We credit the prior work we build on. OpenRank (Karma3) already prototyped verifying a PageRank fixed point on-chain. trustgraphs computes anchored PageRank with zero-knowledge proofs, on EAS rather than ERC-8004. monad-agent-trust studies payment provenance on the same Monad data; that work is complementary, and in our standard it can be plugged in as an additional filter on edges. What ANCRE brings is the combination: anchors chosen by the reader, unmodified ERC-8004 as the data source, certification at the speed of the chain, and a score read by the paying contract itself.

The project was built with heavy use of an AI coding agent (Claude Code), as disclosed in the README. Every number in this description comes from a script in the repository and can be re-run.


GO-TO-MARKET AND USER ACQUISITION

Our first users are the people who lose money when an agent's reputation lies: anyone who pays AI agents automatically. Today that means buyer agents and applications paying for tool calls over x402, agent marketplaces that rank providers, and companies starting to let their own agents spend a budget. They all face the same choice ERC-8004 leaves open: which raters to trust. On Monad mainnet, 83% of the feedback comes from one farm, so the naive answer is already wrong. We do not need to convince these users that the problem exists. We can show it to them on their own chain, and show the payment that ANCRE refuses.

Adoption starts with the standard, not with a sales pitch. ANCRE is the reference implementation of a draft ERC, "Anchored Reputation for Trustless Agents". After the hackathon we will open the pull request on ethereum/ERCs and answer the post in the ERC-8004 discussion thread that asked for reputation relative to reader-chosen anchors. That thread is where the builders of agent infrastructure already gather, so the standard brings us our first integrators. Because the interface is chain-agnostic and ERC-8004 is deployed on more than 40 chains, every integration we win on Monad can be reused elsewhere, while Monad remains the chain where certification is fast and cheap enough to run on every graph change.

We then go where payments are decided. The trust check is one call, isTrusted, and our payer contract wraps it around an x402 payment, so an agent can adopt ANCRE without changing how it pays. We will publish the payer as a drop-in contract, keep the Envio indexer open source, and offer a read API that returns every score together with its certificate, so that nobody has to trust us to use it. Our first target partners are the teams already working on the same data: the Monad x402 facilitator, ERC-8004 explorers such as 8004scan, and the projects that check payment receipts or payment provenance. In our standard, their signals plug in as edge filters, so they gain from ANCRE instead of competing with it.

Cold start is the real challenge, and we plan for it. A trust graph is only useful once honest agents rate each other, and the honest graph on Monad is tiny today. We will seed it with a few public anchor sets whose members and rules are published, and with design partners: teams that run several agents and are willing to rate the providers they actually pay. Every certified payment they make adds an honest edge, so each new partner makes the graph more useful for the next one. Companies get the same product for their own perimeter: a private anchor set turns any public registry into their own trust domain, with no private infrastructure to run.

The business model follows the cost. Reading a score is free for everyone. The owner of an anchor set pays for its certification, which on Monad costs little enough to repeat on every change of the graph, and we will charge a fee for operating that service, acting as a reputation oracle whose every answer can be verified on-chain. We will measure success by the number of anchor sets certified, the number of payments that go through a trust check, and the number of honest edges in the graph, rather than by the number of agents registered.


PROGRESS UPDATE

As of 5 October, the core of ANCRE is built and tested end to end on forks of Monad mainnet and testnet, against the real ERC-8004 registries. The verifier contract accepts certificates checked edge by edge against the registry, and the trust-gated payer refuses x402 payments to agents below threshold. The PageRank solver produces certificates that the contract accepts with identical results. The Envio indexer is live on Envio Cloud for Monad mainnet, Monad testnet and Optimism and matches the solver exactly. The MetaMask Agent Wallet plugin works in the real mm client, with an override that only a human can use. The draft ERC is written and passes the official linter.

Our measurements on mainnet confirm the problem we set out to solve: 83% of the feedback comes from a single farm whose target has a naive average of 1.53e36, and under ANCRE its trust is zero.

This week we deploy the contracts on Monad testnet, run a real payment through the x402 facilitator, then do a minimal run on mainnet. We are also building the live demo: a Sybil farm attacking a provider while ANCRE's score does not move, and a "circuit breaker" where one anchor's veto stops every payer at the next block. After that come the demo screen, the three-minute video and the public repository. One limit we will state openly: the honest graph on mainnet is still tiny, so the positive part of the demo uses our own agent traffic.
